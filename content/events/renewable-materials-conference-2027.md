## Event Overview

The Renewable Materials Conference (RMC) 2027, organised by nova-Institut, returns to the Maternushaus in Cologne after a three-year intermezzo in Siegburg. RMC is the leading global event on the renewable carbon economy, showcasing the latest progress in and prospects of bioeconomy, CCU and recycling for the defossilisation of chemicals and materials.

The conference presents all sustainable and renewable material solutions at one event — bio-based, CO2-based and recycled — as a hybrid event expecting 300 to 400 participants. The 2026 edition drew 365 participants from 26 countries, including large enterprises and brands (39%), research institutes and universities (17%), start-ups and scale-ups (15%), SMEs (11%), associations and NGOs (8%), policymakers (8%) and investors (3%).

## Topics & Focus Areas

- **Bio-based Polymers & Materials**: PLA, PHA, bio-PE, bio-PA, starch blends and novel biopolymers
- **CO2-based Materials**: Power-to-X chemicals and polymers utilizing captured carbon
- **Chemical Recycling**: Mass-balance approaches, pyrolysis and dissolution routes
- **Defossilisation Strategy**: Renewable carbon as the frame for chemicals and materials policy
- **Sustainability Assessment**: LCA, carbon footprint and certification of renewable materials
- **Markets & Investment**: Brand adoption, offtake agreements and financing renewable-carbon ventures

## Why Attend

- **Innovators & Start-ups**: Present renewable-carbon technologies to brands, investors and policymakers
- **Brand Owners & FMCG**: Scout ready-to-use sustainable material solutions across all feedstock types
- **Investors**: Identify scale-up opportunities across the renewable carbon economy
- **Policy & NGOs**: Engage with the industries shaping defossilisation pathways

## Typical Attendance

RMC attracts 300-400 professionals: brand owners (Procter & Gamble, Henkel, dm, Lego, Michelin, Volvo have attended), chemical and polymer producers (BASF, Covestro, Novamont, Braskem, Futerro), start-ups, research institutes, policymakers (European Commission, national ministries) and investors.
