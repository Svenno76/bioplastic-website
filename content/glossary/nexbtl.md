**NExBTL** is the proprietary technology of Finland's **[Neste](/companies/neste/)** for producing renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) from renewable oils and fats. The name stands for the process family Neste developed and commercialized starting in the 2000s, and it underpins Neste's position as one of the world's largest renewable diesel and SAF producers.

## Process Chemistry

NExBTL is a **hydroprocessing** (HVO — hydrotreated vegetable oil) route:

1. **Feedstock pretreatment** — removal of contaminants from vegetable oils, used cooking oil, animal fats, and residue oils.
2. **Hydrotreatment** — the triglycerides in the oils are reacted with hydrogen at elevated temperature and pressure over a catalyst, splitting them into hydrocarbon chains and removing oxygen as water and CO2.
3. **Isomerization** — the straight-chain hydrocarbons are partially isomerized to branched molecules, lowering the pour point so the fuel remains liquid in cold climates.

Unlike transesterification (the route to conventional biodiesel/FAME), hydroprocessing yields a **pure hydrocarbon fuel** that is chemically almost identical to fossil diesel — a true drop-in fuel requiring no engine modifications and no blending limits.

## Feedstocks

Neste has progressively moved NExBTL production away from virgin vegetable oils toward waste and residue streams:

- Used cooking oil (UCO)
- Animal fats from food industry residues
- Vegetable oil residues and technical-grade oils
- Ongoing development of novel feedstocks such as lignocellulosic residues and polymer-derived inputs

## Significance for Bioplastics

Although NExBTL is a fuel technology, it matters to the bioplastics industry in two ways. First, it demonstrates at industrial scale the economics of waste-based feedstock supply chains (collection of used cooking oil, residue oils) that bioplastic producers also depend on. Second, chemical and fuel companies such as Neste are expanding into **polymers and chemicals from renewable feedstocks**, so their feedstock and hydroprocessing expertise increasingly overlaps with bio-based material production.
