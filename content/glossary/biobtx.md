**BioBTX B.V.** is a Dutch technology company, based in Groningen and spun out of research at the University of Groningen, that converts plastic waste and biomass into **BTX aromatics** — benzene, toluene, and xylenes.

## Why BTX Matters

BTX aromatics are fundamental building blocks of the chemical industry: benzene leads to styrene (polystyrene), toluene to polyurethanes and solvents, and paraxylene to **terephthalic acid (PTA)**, the monomer of PET polyester. Today virtually all BTX is produced from fossil naphtha in catalytic reformers. A renewable BTX source therefore de-fossilizes a whole family of downstream plastics, including PET — one of the largest polymer markets in the world.

## Technology

BioBTX's core process is **catalytic pyrolysis / pyrolysis-based aromatization**:

1. **Feedstock conversion** — mixed plastic waste, biomass, or a mixture of both is pyrolyzed at high temperature.
2. **Catalytic aromatization** — the pyrolysis vapors pass over proprietary zeolite-based catalysts that restructure the hydrocarbon fragments into aromatic rings.
3. **Separation** — benzene, toluene, and xylenes are recovered as refinery-grade products.

A distinctive feature is the ability to co-process plastic waste with lignocellulosic biomass, which helps manage the oxygen and chlorine content of the feed and lowers the carbon intensity of the aromatics produced.

## Commercial Development

BioBTX operates a pilot facility in the Netherlands and has been scaling the technology toward demonstration and commercial plants, working with petrochemical partners on integrating renewable BTX into existing cracker and refinery infrastructure. The company's ICCP (Integrated Cascading Catalytic Pyrolysis) process has been demonstrated with real waste plastics at industrially relevant scale.
