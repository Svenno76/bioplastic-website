**Ecovia Bio** is a United States-based biotechnology company that produces **gamma polyglutamic acid (γ-PGA)**, a naturally occurring, bio-based and biodegradable biopolymer, using microbial fermentation. The company aims to replace fossil-based polymers and difficult-to-recycle materials in applications such as agriculture, coatings, and consumer products.

## Technology

γ-PGA is a polymer of the amino acid glutamic acid that is naturally produced by certain *Bacillus* bacteria, notably the strains used in the Japanese food natto. Ecovia Bio manufactures it industrially through **fermentation**: microorganisms convert renewable carbohydrate feedstocks into the biopolymer in fermentation tanks, in a process analogous to industrial amino acid or enzyme production.

Properties that distinguish γ-PGA include:

- **Biodegradability** — the polymer breaks down through microbial action rather than persisting in the environment.
- **Water solubility and film-forming ability** — useful for coatings, seed treatments, and agricultural films.
- **Renewable carbon content** — 100% bio-based when produced from plant-derived sugars.

## Applications

- **Agriculture** — seed coatings, soil conditioners, and controlled-release carriers for fertilizers and crop-protection agents.
- **Personal care and cosmetics** — humectant and film-forming ingredient.
- **Packaging and coatings** — biodegradable films and barrier layers.

## Market Context

Ecovia Bio is part of a growing cohort of companies using precision fermentation to produce novel biopolymers — a category that includes PHA producers and protein-based materials — targeting applications where conventional bioplastics such as PLA or starch blends are not technically or economically suitable.
