**PEF (polyethylene furanoate)** is a 100% plant-based polyester made from **FDCA (2,5-furandicarboxylic acid)** and monoethylene glycol. Because its furan ring is a rigid aromatic structure, PEF offers measurably better gas and moisture barrier properties than PET — roughly six to ten times better oxygen barrier and two times better CO2 barrier — while remaining processable on standard PET-type equipment.

## Key Properties

| Property | PEF | PET |
|----------|-----|-----|
| Oxygen barrier | ~6–10x better | baseline |
| CO2 barrier | ~2x better | baseline |
| Water vapor barrier | better | baseline |
| Glass transition (Tg) | ~86 °C | ~78 °C |
| Tensile strength | higher | baseline |
| Bio-based carbon | up to 100% | 0% (fossil) |

## Production

PEF is produced by polycondensing FDCA with monoethylene glycol. FDCA — the aromatic diacid that replaces PET's petrochemical terephthalic acid — is made by catalytic dehydration and oxidation of plant-based sugars such as fructose. Dutch company **[Avantium](/companies/avantium/)** has led PEF's industrialization with its proprietary **YXY®** catalytic process, a pilot plant in Geleen, and a demonstration-scale FDCA plant in Delfzijl, Netherlands. Avantium markets PEF under the **releaf®** brand.

## Applications

- **Beverage bottles** — small-format bottles for juices, dairy-alternatives, and functional drinks benefit most from the barrier and lightweighting potential.
- **Films and trays** — barrier packaging for oxygen-sensitive food.
- **Textiles and fibers** — PEF fibers for polyester-type applications.

## End-of-Life

PEF is a durable, recyclable polyester rather than a biodegradable one. It is designed to be recyclable in the existing **PET recycling stream**, where PEF/PET blends have been validated as compatible (RecyClass approval), and it can also be chemically recycled by depolymerization back to FDCA and MEG.

## Market Context

PEF is frequently cited as the most advanced 100% bio-based drop-in-style challenger to PET, a polymer market worth well over $100 billion annually. Its commercialization depends on the cost curve of bio-based FDCA: Avantium's Delfzijl plant targets the first industrial-scale FDCA production, and offtake agreements with brand owners in beverages and packaging are the main commercialization pathway.
