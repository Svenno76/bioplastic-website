**BlueAlp** is a Dutch chemical recycling technology company that converts mixed plastic waste into **pyrolysis oil** — a liquid hydrocarbon feedstock that can replace fossil naphtha in the production of new plastics. The company positions its technology as a route to circular plastics for waste streams that mechanical recycling cannot process, such as mixed polyolefin packaging, multi-layer films, and contaminated material.

## Technology

BlueAlp's process is based on **thermal pyrolysis**: plastic waste is heated to high temperatures in the absence of oxygen, breaking the polymer chains down into shorter hydrocarbon molecules. The resulting pyrolysis oil is then purified and can be fed into steam crackers operated by petrochemical partners, where it re-enters the production of virgin-quality polymers such as PE and PP.

Key characteristics of the approach include:

- **Feedstock flexibility** — mixed, contaminated, and multi-layer plastic waste that would otherwise be incinerated or landfilled.
- **Mass-balance certification** — output can be certified under schemes such as ISCC PLUS, allowing brand owners to attribute recycled content to specific products.
- **Modular plant design** — units intended to be replicated at waste-source locations to reduce logistics costs.

## Market Context

BlueAlp operates in a rapidly growing European chemical-recycling sector driven by the EU's **Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR)**, which mandates recycled-content quotas for plastic packaging from 2030. Partnerships with petrochemical producers and waste management companies are central to the business model, linking waste collection streams to cracker capacity.
