The **circular economy in bioplastics** describes closed-loop material systems in which bio-based plastics are produced, used, collected, and reprocessed — or safely biodegraded — so that their carbon remains in productive use instead of being lost to incineration, landfill, or the environment.

## Two Loops

Bio-based plastics participate in two complementary loops:

- **The technical loop** — durable bio-based plastics (bio-PET, bio-PE, PLA in well-sorted streams) are collected and mechanically or chemically recycled back into polymers, displacing fossil feedstocks. See [recycled content](/glossary/recycled-content/).
- **The biological loop** — biodegradable bioplastics (such as PHA, starch blends, or compostable PBAT) are recovered through **organic recycling**: industrial composting or anaerobic digestion, where the polymer breaks down into biomass, CO2, and water that re-enter the biosphere. See [composting infrastructure](/glossary/composting-infrastructure/).

## Design Rules

For a bioplastic to close a loop in practice, several conditions must hold:

1. **Application-appropriate material choice** — biodegradability only helps where items are likely to end up in organic waste streams (food caddy liners, fruit stickers, tea bags, mulch films); everywhere else, recyclability or durability is the better goal.
2. **Sortability** — the material must be identifiable in sorting facilities, or it contaminates existing recycling streams.
3. **Verified end-of-life claims** — certified standards such as **EN 13432** (industrial compostability) define what "compostable" actually means; claims without certification are a key greenwashing risk.
4. **Real collection infrastructure** — a loop only closes if composting or recycling capacity actually receives the material.

## Regulatory Context

EU policy is the main driver: the **Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR)** sets recycled-content quotas and design-for-recycling requirements, and the **EU Taxonomy** and **Single-Use Plastics Directive** shape which materials count as sustainable. Bio-based carbon content additionally offers GHG advantages under LCA-based reporting frameworks.
