**PET (polyethylene terephthalate)** is a clear, strong, lightweight polyester thermoplastic and one of the highest-volume commodity plastics in the world. It is best known as the material of beverage bottles, food jars and trays, and polyester fibers for textiles.

## Chemistry and Production

PET is a condensation polymer of two monomers:

- **Purified terephthalic acid (PTA)** or its dimethyl ester (DMT) — the aromatic dicarboxylic acid component
- **Monoethylene glycol (MEG)** — the diol component

Both monomers are today overwhelmingly fossil-derived (PTA from paraxylene, MEG from ethylene). The two are esterified and then polycondensated at high temperature under vacuum, driving off water and building molecular weight. PET's benzene rings give it stiffness, clarity, and good barrier properties; its glass transition temperature of about 78 °C and melting point of about 250 °C define its processing window.

## Applications

- **Beverage bottles** — the dominant application; PET's clarity, CO2 barrier, and safety record made it the global standard for carbonated soft drinks and water.
- **Food packaging** — trays, clamshells, jars, films (often as biaxially oriented PET film, e.g. Mylar).
- **Textiles** — polyester fiber is the world's most-produced synthetic fiber.
- **Engineering plastics** — glass-filled PET grades for automotive and electrical parts.

## Bio-Based and Recycled PET

Three sustainable variants matter in the bioplastics context:

- **Bio-PET (partially bio-based)** — replaces fossil MEG with plant-derived MEG (30% bio-based carbon), or, in fully bio-based routes under development, also fossil PTA with bio-based paraxylene. See [bio-PET](/glossary/bio-pet/).
- **rPET (mechanically recycled)** — post-consumer bottles are collected, sorted, washed, and re-granulated; rPET is the most established recycled plastic in food contact. See [rPET](/glossary/rpet-recycled-pet/).
- **PEF (the furan alternative)** — polyethylene furanoate replaces PET's benzene ring with a plant-derived furan ring, delivering substantially better barrier properties. It is chemically similar enough to be recyclable in the PET stream. See [PEF](/glossary/pef/).

## Recycling and End-of-Life

PET is the most successfully recycled commodity plastic, with well-established bottle deposit and collection systems in many regions. It can be mechanically recycled several times; chemical recycling (glycolysis, methanolysis, enzymatic hydrolysis) is being commercialized to recycle colored, multilayer, and fiber-grade PET that mechanical recycling cannot handle. Under the EU's Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, PET beverage bottles must contain at least 25% recycled content from 2025 and 30% from 2030.
