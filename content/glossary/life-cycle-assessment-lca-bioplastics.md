**Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) for bioplastics** is the application of the standardized LCA methodology (ISO 14040/14044) to bio-based plastics. It quantifies environmental impacts across the full life cycle — feedstock cultivation, conversion, use, and end-of-life — and is the basis for most sustainability claims made about bioplastics.

## What Makes Bioplastic LCA Different

Assessing a bio-based plastic is more complex than assessing a fossil one:

- **Biogenic carbon** — the CO2 a plant absorbed during growth must be tracked through the product's life and released (or stored) at end-of-life, in either the technical or biological loop.
- **Land use and agriculture** — cultivation brings fertilizer emissions, water use, and direct/indirect land-use change (ILUC) impacts that fossil plastics do not have.
- **Co-product allocation** — biorefineries produce multiple outputs (feed, fuel, chemicals), and how environmental burdens are split among them (allocation vs. system expansion) can shift results significantly.
- **End-of-life modeling** — compostable plastics need composting scenarios; durable bio-plastics need recycling or energy-recovery scenarios.

## Typical Findings

Meta-studies consistently show that substituting fossil plastics with today's bioplastics usually reduces **greenhouse gas emissions** (often by a meaningful margin, depending on feedstock and energy mix) and reduces **fossil resource depletion**, while impacts such as **eutrophication** and **acidification** can be equal or higher because of agriculture. Whether a bioplastic "wins" therefore depends on the impact category, the feedstock, and the energy system assumed.

## Standards and Benchmarks

- **ISO 14040/14044** — the methodological backbone.
- **Product Environmental Footprint (PEF)** — the EU's harmonized method, used in policy contexts.
- **ASTM D6866 / EN 16640** — measure biobased carbon content, an input to but not a substitute for LCA.
- **Cradle-to-gate vs. cradle-to-grave** — most published bioplastics LCAs are cradle-to-gate; end-of-life assumptions dominate cradle-to-grave results.

## Why It Matters

LCA is the gatekeeper of credibility in the bioplastics debate: green claims without a transparent, third-party-reviewed LCA are increasingly challenged by regulators (e.g. under the EU Green Claims initiative) and by NGO scrutiny.
