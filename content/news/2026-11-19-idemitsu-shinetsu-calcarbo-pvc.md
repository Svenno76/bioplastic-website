Idemitsu Kosan and Shin-Etsu Chemical have jointly developed a PVC material compounded with Calcarbo, a synthetic calcium carbonate produced by fixing carbon dioxide captured from combustion exhaust gas. The two Japanese chemical companies are extending the material's use beyond the construction products where it had remained confined into everyday goods such as notebooks and stationery.

Calcarbo is a synthetic calcium carbonate made by fixing CO2 from boiler exhaust onto calcium recovered from concrete slurry, the waste left over from the final stages of concrete product manufacturing. The filler turns two waste streams, emitted CO2 and concrete sludge, into a mineral component of a durable polymer, embedding captured carbon in everyday products.

## From building materials to everyday goods

Until now the use of Calcarbo had been confined largely to construction materials. In the joint development, Idemitsu supplied the carbon-recycled filler while Shin-Etsu, one of the world's largest PVC producers, handled the design and evaluation of the PVC compounds. The first commercial result is a PVC leather for notebooks and stationery products, produced by Maruki Chemical Industry, a Shin-Etsu affiliate.

The partners position the material as one that reduces CO2 emissions and grows the business at the same time, and plan to extend the Calcarbo PVC combination into a wider range of everyday goods, moving carbon recycling from construction materials into products of daily use. PVC is one of the highest-volume commodity plastics worldwide, so filler systems that reduce its net carbon burden while preserving processability carry leverage beyond the niche volumes typical of many bio-based alternatives.
