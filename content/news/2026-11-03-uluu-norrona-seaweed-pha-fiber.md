Uluu, the Perth-based materials startup producing PHA from seaweed, has signed a partnership with Norwegian outdoor apparel brand Norrøna to develop a seaweed-derived PHA performance fibre for use in technical outdoor clothing.

The agreement marks Uluu's first move into textiles, extending its PHA platform beyond the packaging applications it has pursued to date. Under the partnership, the two companies will develop fibre samples and test them in performance outdoor garments, with the goal of demonstrating that seaweed-based PHA can meet the demanding mechanical and durability requirements of technical apparel.

For Norrøna, the partnership offers a route to reduce the reliance of its product range on virgin polyester and nylon, which dominate the outdoor sector but are derived from fossil feedstocks and shed microfibres during washing. PHA fibres are expected to offer a lower-carbon alternative that is also less prone to microfibre persistence in the environment.

For Uluu, the deal represents a validation of its seaweed-fed PHA platform in a new vertical and provides a reference customer in a premium brand known for exacting material standards. The company has been scaling production of its PHA at pilot facilities in Western Australia as it works toward first commercial volumes.
