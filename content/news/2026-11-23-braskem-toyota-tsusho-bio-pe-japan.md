Braskem and Toyota Tsusho, the trading company of the Toyota Group, are expanding their partnership on sugarcane-derived bio-based polyethylene in Japan, positioning the resin as a drop-in alternative to fossil-based PE that cuts packaging-related greenhouse gas emissions without a performance penalty.

The two companies present Japan as the most advanced market in East Asia for bio-based materials, supported by what they describe as a strong policy and certification framework for biomass plastics. Adoption is spreading across shopping bags, food packaging and everyday consumer products, with cosmetics and personal care packaging emerging as the strongest growth segment, in bottles, tubes, caps and closures.

## From first adoption to higher content

"Japan has played a pioneering role in building demand, supported by a strong policy and certification framework for biomass plastics," said Frederico Campos, Braskem's business director for Asia Pacific. "Our focus there is to help partners move from initial adoption to higher bio-based content across more packaging applications." Campos added that brands in the region increasingly evaluate packaging through a product carbon footprint lens, creating demand for materials that deliver measurable lifecycle reductions while keeping performance and recyclability.

The commercial argument leans on a University of Tokyo life-cycle assessment, presented at Highly Functional Material Week Tokyo 2026 by Toyota Tsusho, which calculated greenhouse gas emissions from sugarcane cultivation through to final incineration and found plant-based PE emits less over its full life cycle. Braskem's Carbon Reduction Calculator is being used to make those savings concrete and comparable for individual products.

## A template for other Asian markets

Braskem's sugarcane PE captures CO2 as the cane grows, giving the material what the company describes as a negative cradle-to-gate carbon footprint while preserving the performance and recyclability of conventional PE. The company reports growing interest from China, South Korea and Australia as well, where brands need credible pathways to emissions targets, and says it is working with local converters there to accelerate qualification and launches on the Japanese template.
