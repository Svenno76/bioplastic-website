The US Department of Energy has reinstated its award to Eastman Chemical Company supporting the build-out of domestic recycled plastics supply chains, restoring a federal funding commitment that had been among the industrial decarbonization awards reviewed in earlier federal sweeps.

## What the award funds

The award backs Eastman's molecular recycling programme, which uses methanolysis to break down polyester plastics — including coloured and mixed streams that mechanical recycling cannot handle — into their constituent monomers for reuse in new polyester. The company's commercial-scale methanolysis facility operates in Kingsport, Tennessee.

## The policy signal

Reinstatement matters for more than the balance sheet. It signals that molecular recycling has regained political standing as a domestic-manufacturing and circularity lever — a signal that lands alongside state-level recycled-content mandates and the EU's packaging regulation as demand-pull drivers for the plastics-circularity sector.

For Eastman, the restored funding line supports a supply-chain argument the company has made consistently: molecular recycling converts waste streams that mechanical systems struggle with, and domestic capacity shortens the supply chain for brands racing to meet recycled-content commitments.
