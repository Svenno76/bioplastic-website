Biotechnology company Ecopha Biotech has signed a memorandum of understanding with pressure-vessel and bioprocess equipment manufacturer Morimatsu to develop modular biorefineries that co-produce sustainable aviation fuel (SAF), PHA bioplastics, and related bio-based products.

## The partnership

The agreement pairs Ecopha's bioprocess technology with Morimatsu's equipment manufacturing and module fabrication capability. Rather than bespoke stick-built plants, the partnership will develop standardized, prefabricated biorefinery modules that can be replicated at customer sites — an approach that shifts capital From civil engineering toward repeatable equipment.

## Why modular matters for SAF

Modular construction has become the preferred answer to the "first plant cost trap" in emerging bio-technology: full-scale plants carry financing risk that modular, replicable designs diffuse. If module economics hold across successive deployments, each successive unit gets cheaper as learning transfers and supply chains mature — the model proven by modular water-treatment and biogas vendors in prior decades.

## Product slate flexibility

The co-production model — SAF alongside PHA bioplastics and co-products — lets operators flex output toward whichever product carries the best margin in a given quarter. That flexibility addresses the revenue risk that has stalled other first-of-a-kind biofacilities, where a single product's price cycle can make or break project economics.
