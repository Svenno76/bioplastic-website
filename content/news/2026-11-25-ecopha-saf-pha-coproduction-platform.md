Ecopha Biotech, the Melbourne-headquartered biotechnology company, is advancing an integrated biorefinery model that converts non-food biomass into two complementary outputs: sustainable aviation fuel and biodegradable PHA bioplastics. The company says the dual-product architecture is designed to tackle two decarbonization problems at once, aviation emissions and the persistence of conventional plastics.

The feedstock portfolio deliberately avoids food crops. It combines pongamia, a hardy non-food oilseed tree cultivated on marginal land, with used cooking oil, food waste and high-yield sorghum, upgrading agricultural residues and recovered organic waste into premium industrial outputs rather than competing with food supply chains.

## One biorefinery, two revenue streams

The commercial logic of the model lies in pairing a bulk fuel with a high-value polymer: SAF is a drop-in aviation fuel that must meet stringent performance and lifecycle carbon standards, while PHA is a naturally biodegradable, compostable polymer positioned as a microplastic-free alternative to fossil plastics in packaging, medical devices, consumer goods and industrial applications. Diversified dual-product revenue streams are intended to strengthen the bankability of biorefinery projects, an economics lesson drawn from single-product plants that have struggled to carry financing alone.

Ecopha frames the co-production model as supporting project bankability through diversified revenue streams, with lifecycle carbon reductions claimed across both the fuel and the polymer side. The company operates as a developer working with feedstock growers, airlines, energy producers, packaging manufacturers and investors to deploy regional biorefinery projects, with its Singapore-based partner Morimatsu already signed on for modular biorefinery concepts. The announcement positions PHA bioplastics for food packaging, medical devices, consumer goods and industrial applications where genuine biodegradability without persistent microplastic residue is the selling point.
