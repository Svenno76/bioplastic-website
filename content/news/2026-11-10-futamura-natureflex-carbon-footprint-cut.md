Specialty films producer Futamura has reported a 24% reduction in the carbon footprint of its NatureFlex cellulose films since 2019, based on a cradle-to-gate life cycle assessment verified by sustainability consultancy ERM.

The improvement reflects a six-year effort across Futamura's production sites to reduce energy intensity and shift energy sourcing, and it arrives at a moment when verified footprint data has become a decisive input in packaging procurement rather than a marketing afterthought.

## A wood-pulp route to flexible packaging

NatureFlex films are made from renewable wood pulp sourced from certified forestry, positioning them as a compostable alternative to conventional plastic films in flexible packaging. The films carry industrial and home composting certifications, and they are used in applications ranging from TwistWrap confectionery to labels and wraparound formats.

## Verified numbers, not projections

The verification by ERM matters because packaging converters and brand owners increasingly ask for independently reviewed life cycle assessments rather than marketing claims when validating formats. Standards such as ISO 14040 and ISO 14044 define how biogenic carbon and end-of-life credits are counted, and a verified 24% reduction gives procurement teams a defensible number to work with.

Verified footprint cuts also position NatureFlex against other renewables-led formats in the transition away from multi-layer plastic films, as incoming packaging regulations in Europe and elsewhere reshape the competitive set for flexible packaging substrates.
