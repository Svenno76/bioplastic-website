Researchers at the University of Michigan, supported by TotalEnergies Corbion experts, have completed an extensive study of Luminy L040 PLA meltblown nonwovens examining how processing conditions shape the structure and performance of PLA air filtration media. The work offers manufacturers a practical framework for producing PLA-based filtration media with performance close to that of conventional polypropylene.

The study established clear relationships between process conditions, fibre dimensions, internal microstructure and the properties that decide filtration performance: efficiency, electrostatic charge retention, thermal stability and shrinkage. Optimized PLA meltblown media achieved filtration performance similar to conventional polypropylene-based materials, and the polymer proved straightforward to electrostatically charge even without specialty charge-enhancing additives.

## Submicron fibres from standard equipment

Two findings stand out for manufacturers. First, Luminy L040 can be processed through specialty dies with extremely fine hole diameters, enabling the submicron fibres that high-efficiency filtration requires. Second, the finest fibres delivered the strongest long-term charge retention, meaning the route to durable performance runs through finer fibre formation rather than additive chemistry.

The research also highlighted the importance of the temperature the material experiences between the die and the collector belt: this stage allows the polymer to develop crystallinity, which reduces shrinkage, improves charge retention and keeps filtration efficiency consistent over time. The trials were conducted on a laboratory-scale production line, so the results now need translation to commercial manufacturing conditions.

## Performance and sustainability alongside each other

"The study bridges the gap between sustainability and performance, demonstrating that PLA can be a viable material choice for next-generation filtration media," said Kristel Beckers, senior application specialist at TotalEnergies Corbion. Meltblown lines have historically been designed around polypropylene, and the study's processing roadmap is intended to lower the barrier for producers experimenting with biobased alternatives.

For TotalEnergies Corbion, the independent validation of its L040 grade, launched specifically for high-efficiency air and water filtration, opens a new application field beyond packaging, one where latency to fossil-based incumbents has historically been justified by performance. The company positions the findings as accelerating the adoption of high-performance filtration media with a reduced carbon footprint.
