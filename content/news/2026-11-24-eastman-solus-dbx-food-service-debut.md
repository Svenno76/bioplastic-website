Eastman has announced the first commercial deployment of Solus DBX-coated food service packaging to consumers in the United States through The Penny Ice Creamery, an independent ice cream operator in Santa Cruz, California, which now serves its ice cream in Solus DBX-coated paper cups across all locations.

Solus DBX is Eastman's biobased coating for paper food service packaging, with certain formulations containing up to 93 percent biobased content. It is designed as an alternative to conventional low-density polyethylene and PLA liners, and can support additional end-of-life pathways for coated paper articles, including industrial composting and paper recycling, depending on article design and local infrastructure.

## Drop-in for converters

A central design goal is converter adoptability: the coating is applied to paper cups, bowls and to-go containers and is engineered to run on existing LDPE coating lines, letting converters adopt the technology without major capital investment. Eastman says the coating delivers the moisture and oil-and-grease barrier performance food service operators require, including the heat sealability validated in kitchen trials with leading national quick-service restaurant brands.

Solus DBX, depending on grade, holds industrial or home compostability certification from TUV Austria and recyclability certification in the paper stream from the Western Michigan University Recycling Institute. Formulations can also incorporate recycled content allocated via ISCC PLUS mass balance through Eastman's recycling technologies.

## First customer, wider rollout ahead

"We're proud to be the first to bring this option to our guests," said Zach Davis, co-owner of The Penny Ice Creamery, whose cups now carry the coating in daily service. Eastman reports that additional converter partnerships are expected to launch in late 2026 and into 2027 as food service operators weigh customer expectations and evolving packaging regulation.

The move puts a compostable-paper cup system into a real US food service operation at commercial scale, a market test for how far certified-compostable coatings can displace polyethylene and PLA liners in quick-service packaging, a category under growing scrutiny from single-use packaging rules in the United States and Europe alike.
