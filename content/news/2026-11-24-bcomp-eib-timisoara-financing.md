The European Investment Bank is providing EUR 20 million in financing to Swiss cleantech company Bcomp to increase production of high-performance natural fibre composite materials. The financing, structured as convertible venture debt backed by the EU's InvestEU programme, supports the construction of a high-volume manufacturing plant in Timisoara, Romania and continued research and development in Fribourg, Switzerland.

Bcomp develops composite materials based primarily on flax fibres as an alternative to conventional carbon fibre, glass fibre and oil-based plastics, serving customers in the automotive, motorsport and recreational equipment industries, including recent serial deployments such as the Polestar 5. The EIB facility supports the company's EUR 47.6 million investment programme running between 2027 and 2030, with around 61 percent of the planned investment taking place in Romania's western Vest region.

## From innovation to industrial scale

The announcement follows Bcomp's own reveal of the new production facility on 18 November. The Timisoara site, on track to launch in 2027, will significantly increase production of roving, the foundational yarn used to weave the company's ampliTex natural fibre fabrics. Bringing roving production in-house is intended to give Bcomp greater quality control and a more resilient, near-shored supply chain close to its European automotive customer base.

"Europe needs to turn technological innovation into industrial production if we are to strengthen our competitiveness and accelerate the green transition," said EIB Vice-President Ioannis Tsakiris. Timisoara was selected for its automotive manufacturing expertise, skilled workforce and proximity to both European OEM customers and Bcomp's Swiss headquarters.

## Scaling the bio-based value chain

The financing addresses a recurring hurdle for cleantech scale-ups: securing risk capital for the step from validated technology to serial production. EIB support covers both the Romanian plant and Bcomp's research and development in Switzerland, and contributes to the bank's TechEU programme for deep-tech innovation across the Union.

"As demand for our natural fibre composites grows, the EIB's support will allow us to expand production and continue investing in R&D, strengthening our ability to serve industrial customers and accelerating the adoption of our high-performance natural fibre materials," said Bcomp chief executive Christian Fischer.
