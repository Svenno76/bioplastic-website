Amsterdam-based Corbion has ended the divestment process for its 50 percent stake in the TotalEnergies Corbion joint venture, the PLA producer it operates alongside the French energy group. The company said the offers received during the sale process did not reflect the value of the business, prompting the decision to retain the asset.

The move removes one of the most closely watched M&A questions in the bioplastics industry. TotalEnergies Corbion operates a 75,000-tonne-per-year PLA plant in Rayong, Thailand, and is one of only a handful of producers worldwide supplying lactide and PLA at commercial scale.

For buyers and brand owners, the decision signals continuity in the supply of Luminy PLA grades, which are used in packaging, compostable foodservice ware, and durable applications. For the market, it suggests that Corbion sees the long-term value of PLA capacity exceeding the prices offered by bidders in a sector that has been marked by cautious valuations.

The announcement comes at a time when PLA demand is being lifted by single-use plastic restrictions in the European Union and other jurisdictions, although producers continue to face pressure from oversupply and price competition. Corbion said it will continue to invest in the joint venture as part of its strategy of growing its sustainable ingredient and materials businesses.
