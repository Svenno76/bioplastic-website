Aduro Clean Technologies has advanced work on a comparative life cycle assessment of its Hydrochemolytic Technology, engaging consultancy Delphi to benchmark the company's plastic-to-chemicals conversion route against conventional virgin production.

The study follows ISO 14040 methodology, the format that offtakers, regulators, and financial partners typically require before accepting environmental claims in offtake agreements, product declarations, or project financing. For a pre-commercial technology, an independent LCA is among the last analytical boxes to tick before customer qualification begins.

Aduro's process uses water in a catalytic medium to convert waste plastics into oils and chemicals that re-enter existing value chains, and the company completed a 120-hour continuous pilot campaign on real post-consumer waste earlier this month — the process-stability evidence on which feedstock and yield assumptions in an LCA rest.

## The evidence stack behind a first plant

The assessment adds an environmental leg to the evidence stack Aduro has been assembling alongside pilot operations: extended-run process data, feedstock partnerships, and engineering groundwork for a first-of-a-kind commercial facility at Chemelot in the Netherlands.

Comparable analyses have proven decisive for other conversion technologies approaching commercialization, where carbon-intensity differential against virgin routes forms the core of the value proposition. Published, third-party-reviewed numbers will let Aduro position its future output against both virgin chemicals and pyrolysis-based competitors.

The company has not given a completion date. Results are expected to feed offtake negotiations and the financing package for the Chemelot facility.
