Aduro Clean Technologies, the Canadian developer of chemical recycling solutions for waste plastics, has successfully completed a 120-hour continuous run of its hydrothermal catalytic conversion process on real post-consumer plastic waste at its pilot facility in Sarnia, Ontario.

The campaign, the longest continuous run the company has achieved to date, marks a significant step in the de-risking of Aduro's technology platform as it works toward commercial deployment. Hydrothermal processes use water at elevated temperatures and pressures as a reaction medium, offering a route to convert plastic waste into valuable oils and chemicals without the high-temperature pyrolysis typically used in chemical recycling.

Extended continuous operation is a critical milestone for any pre-commercial chemical process, as it demonstrates not only that the core chemistry works but that the equipment, catalysts, and process control systems can sustain stable operation over industrially meaningful time periods.

Aduro's hydrothermal catalytic conversion process is designed to handle contaminated and mixed plastic waste streams that are difficult to process mechanically, positioning it as a complement to existing mechanical recycling infrastructure rather than a competitor to it.
