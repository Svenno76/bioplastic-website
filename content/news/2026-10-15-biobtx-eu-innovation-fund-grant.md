BioBTX, the Groningen-based chemical technology company, has been awarded a grant from the EU Innovation Fund to support the construction of its first commercial-scale plant converting mixed plastic waste into BTX aromatics, the building blocks of the chemical industry.

The grant, one of the most competitive funding instruments in the European climate and energy landscape, will support the plant, which is designed to convert approximately 20,000 tonnes of plastic waste per year into valuable aromatic chemicals currently sourced almost exclusively from fossil feedstocks.

BioBTX's technology uses a catalytic process to convert plastic waste pyrolysis oil into benzene, toluene, and xylenes, the aromatic molecules that serve as the foundation for a wide range of plastics, solvents, and other chemical products. The company has been operating a pilot plant in Groningen and has now secured the funding needed to advance to its first commercial-scale facility.

The award reflects growing EU support for technologies that close the loop on plastic waste by converting it into virgin-grade chemical feedstocks rather than downcycling it into lower-value applications.
