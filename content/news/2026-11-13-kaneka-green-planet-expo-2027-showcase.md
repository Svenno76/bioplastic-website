Kaneka, the Japanese chemicals producer and one of the world's largest PHA manufacturers, used its presence at Green x Expo 2027 in Tokyo to place its Green Planet polyhydroxyalkanoate (PHA) family at the centre of its materials showcase.

## From premium ingredient to volume material

The company's exhibit focused on applications rather than chemistry: PHA-based compounds are moving from premium, sustainability-led product lines into mainstream categories as production capacity expands. Kaneka has invested in scale-up at its Takasago and Tokushima sites, and the showcase framed Green Planet as a material for volume markets rather than niche eco-products.

## The end-of-life argument

Green Planet is produced via fermentation, and the company emphasised its biodegradation credentials — the material breaks down in seawater, soil, and compost environments, a property set that differentiates it from PLA and starch blends. Kaneka has published data showing breakdown in temperate seawater, a claim it connects to growing concern over plastic persistence in oceans.

## The scale question

Kaneka's showcase reflected a production capacity in the several-thousand-tonne range at its Japanese sites, with the company working toward tens of thousands of tonnes as demand matures. The pitch to converters at the Expo concentrated on applications where marine-degradable PHA commands a premium or answers a regulatory driver: food service, agriculture, and packaging formats where composting infrastructure cannot be assumed.

With Asian and European regulators moving against conventional plastics in specific formats, Kaneka's framing positioned PHA not as an environmental niche but as an incumbent-competitive material whose moment arrives with capacity.
