UPM, the Finnish forest industry company, has inaugurated its first commercial-scale biorefinery at Leuna in Saxony-Anhalt, Germany, a facility designed to convert sustainably sourced solid wood into a range of renewable chemical building blocks.

The plant uses a modified version of the company's proprietary technology to produce bio-based naphtha and bio-paraffin alongside a renewable diesel component, all derived from residual wood streams sourced from sustainably managed forests. These outputs can then be used either as fuels or, critically for the bioplastics sector, as feedstock for the production of bio-based polyethylene, polypropylene, and other polymers through established cracking and polymerisation processes.

For the European bioplastics industry, the Leuna facility is significant because it provides a scalable, certified source of bio-based hydrocarbon feedstock that can be processed in existing petrochemical infrastructure without modification. This drop-in compatibility means brand owners can introduce certified renewable content into existing packaging and product lines without reformulating or requalifying materials.

The inauguration was attended by German and Finnish government officials, reflecting the strategic importance both countries place on building out domestic renewable chemical capacity.
